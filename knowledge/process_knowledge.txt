PHARMA MANUFACTURING PROCESS KNOWLEDGE BASE
===========================================

[KB-001] TEMPERATURE CONTROL

Temperature is an important process parameter in pharmaceutical manufacturing.
Temperature excursions may influence reaction kinetics, degradation pathways,
impurity formation, physical properties, and overall process performance.

When abnormal temperature is observed, investigation may include:
- temperature sensor and calibration status
- heating and cooling system performance
- temperature excursion duration
- batch temperature profile
- mixing and heat-transfer conditions
- comparison with historical successful batches

A statistical or machine-learning association between temperature and product
quality does not by itself establish that temperature caused a batch failure.


[KB-002] pH CONTROL

pH may influence reaction behavior, chemical stability, solubility,
degradation pathways, and impurity formation.

When unusual pH behavior is observed, investigation may include:
- pH meter calibration
- sampling procedure
- reagent addition
- buffer preparation
- process-stage pH profile
- comparison with historical batches

The importance of pH can be nonlinear. Therefore, weak linear correlation
does not necessarily mean that pH has no predictive relevance.


[KB-003] INTERMEDIATE IMPURITY

Intermediate impurity level can provide information about process performance
and product-quality risk.

Elevated impurity may be associated with:
- incomplete conversion
- degradation
- side reactions
- raw-material variability
- process-condition excursions
- downstream removal limitations

Investigation should consider impurity trends together with relevant process
conditions rather than interpreting impurity in isolation.


[KB-004] RAW MATERIAL PURITY

Raw-material purity can influence process consistency and downstream product
quality.

When unusual raw-material behavior is suspected, investigation may include:
- incoming material test results
- certificate-of-analysis review
- supplier and lot information
- historical lot performance
- storage conditions
- material age
- deviations from expected purity

A predictive relationship with raw-material purity should be treated as
supporting evidence and not automatic proof of supplier or material failure.


[KB-005] REACTOR EFFECT

Differences between reactors may reflect equipment-specific or operational
conditions.

Potential investigation areas include:
- reactor qualification status
- calibration
- mixing performance
- heat-transfer behavior
- maintenance history
- equipment configuration
- cleaning history
- historical reactor performance

A reactor association identified by statistical analysis or machine learning
does not establish equipment failure.


[KB-006] SUPPLIER EFFECT

Supplier-related differences can sometimes reflect variability in material
properties, specifications, manufacturing processes, storage, or transportation.

Investigation may include:
- supplier lot comparison
- incoming quality results
- certificate-of-analysis review
- historical supplier performance
- storage and transportation conditions
- change-control history

Supplier association should not be interpreted as supplier failure without
supporting investigation evidence.


[KB-007] PROCESS TIME

Process time may affect conversion, degradation, impurity formation, or
overall process consistency.

Investigation may include:
- actual versus expected process duration
- hold times
- processing interruptions
- relationship with temperature and pH
- historical process-time distributions

Process time should normally be evaluated together with other process
parameters.


[KB-008] MODEL EXPLAINABILITY

Feature importance identifies variables that are useful to a predictive model.

SHAP values explain how individual features contribute to a specific model
prediction relative to the model baseline.

SHAP values describe model behavior. They do not establish physical or
scientific causation.

A large SHAP contribution means that the model relied strongly on that
feature for the specific prediction.


[KB-009] MODEL PREDICTION

Machine-learning predictions should be treated as decision-support evidence.

A low predicted quality score can be used to prioritize investigation but
should not independently determine pharmaceutical batch disposition,
release, rejection, or root cause.

Model predictions should be interpreted together with experimental,
analytical, process, and quality-system evidence.


[KB-010] ROOT-CAUSE GUARDRAIL

Predictive models identify patterns and associations present in historical
data.

The following distinction must be maintained:

MODEL EVIDENCE:
A variable contributed strongly to a prediction.

SCIENTIFIC CONCLUSION:
A variable caused the observed manufacturing outcome.

Moving from model evidence to a causal conclusion requires additional
scientific investigation and supporting evidence.


[KB-011] INVESTIGATION PRIORITIZATION

When a model predicts poor quality, investigation should prioritize variables
with strong negative model contributions while also considering relevant
process knowledge.

A practical investigation sequence may include:
1. identify the strongest model contributors
2. compare observed values with historical successful batches
3. verify measurement and calibration status
4. review related process parameters
5. examine raw-material and equipment history
6. review analytical evidence
7. determine whether additional experiments or investigation are required

Model evidence should guide investigation prioritization rather than replace
scientific judgment.


[KB-012] DECISION-SUPPORT BOUNDARY

The AI system is intended to support process understanding and investigation.

It should not independently:
- establish manufacturing root cause
- approve or reject a pharmaceutical batch
- override validated specifications
- replace quality-system procedures
- claim causal relationships solely from predictive-model output

Final manufacturing and quality decisions require appropriate scientific and
quality review.
